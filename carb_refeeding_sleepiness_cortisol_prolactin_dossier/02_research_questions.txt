PRIMARY QUESTIONS
1. Does prolonged carbohydrate restriction produce a human state of reduced tolerance to carbohydrate refeeding, and if so by what mechanisms?
2. How often does low-carbohydrate adaptation alter skeletal-muscle glucose uptake machinery (GLUT4, insulin receptor, PDK4, AMPK), and on what timescale do these changes appear and attenuate [S001, S002]?
3. How do caloric deficit and fasting alter baseline cortisol concentration, pulse frequency, and circadian timing in humans [S006]?
4. Is elevated fasting prolactin a reproducible feature of fasting/caloric restriction, or is it context-, assay-, obesity-, or challenge-dependent [S003, S007]?
5. Why can carbohydrate refeeding provoke somnolence? Which pathways are best supported: reactive hypoglycemia, orexin suppression, serotonin/melatonin, parasympathetic activation, inflammatory response, or others [S004, S005, S010]?
6. How do cortisol and carbohydrate ingestion interact temporally? Can carbohydrate ingestion reduce a stress-elevated arousal state and thereby unmask sleepiness?
7. Could the reported symptom reflect true biochemical reactive hypoglycemia, or only rapid decline in glucose without documented hypoglycemia [S004]?
8. What role do high protein and the specific fatty-acid profile play in modifying glucose response, arousal, inflammation, and hormone signaling [S005, S011]?
9. How strong is the evidence for GLA/PGE1-prolactin/cortisol interaction claims versus their historical or speculative status [S011]?
10. What contradictory evidence exists, and under what boundary conditions do proposed mechanisms fail?

SECONDARY QUESTIONS
11. Does low-carbohydrate dieting induce a low-T3 or euthyroid-sick-like adaptation, and could that reduce resilience to postprandial sedation [S008, S009]?
12. Are meal timing and circadian alignment important moderators of cortisol rhythms and postprandial sleepiness [S006, S010]?
13. Do EPA/DHA reduce postprandial inflammatory signaling enough to offset refeeding fatigue mechanisms [S005, S011]?
14. Is elevated prolactin acting mainly through dopamine/arousal, through metabolic effects, or as a marker of broader hypothalamic stress signaling [S003]?
15. Are there analogous clinical situations—bariatric reactive hypoglycemia, postprandial somnolence, insulin-resistant states—that provide useful mechanistic parallels [S004]?

RED-TEAM QUESTIONS
16. Could the whole pattern be explained without prolactin at all?
17. Could excessive sleepiness be due mainly to sleep debt, circadian misalignment, or underfeeding rather than carbohydrate per se?
18. Could the issue be exaggerated insulin secretion without true metabolic inflexibility?
19. Could the fatty-acid profile protect against, rather than worsen, some candidate mechanisms?
20. What findings would overturn the current leading model?
