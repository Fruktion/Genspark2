ASSUMPTIONS CURRENTLY BEING MADE
1. The reported carbohydrate restriction was long enough to produce meaningful fuel-adaptation and tissue-level glucose-handling changes.
2. Elevated fasting cortisol is a real and repeated observation rather than a single stress-contaminated assay.
3. Elevated fasting prolactin is real, but its causal meaning is less certain.
4. The symptom is meaningfully caused by carbohydrate exposure and not just by meal size, circadian timing, or total calories.
5. The specific fatty-acid profile matters, but probably as a modifier rather than the sole driver.

OPEN QUESTIONS
1. Was actual hypoglycemia documented at the time of sleepiness?
2. Did sleepiness occur with pure carbohydrate, mixed meals, or all carb-containing meals?
3. Is the symptom dose-dependent with carbohydrate amount or glycemic index?
4. Were thyroid markers (T3/rT3), catecholamines, leptin, and ketones measured alongside cortisol/prolactin?
5. Are there hidden confounders such as sleep restriction, overtraining, stimulant withdrawal, circadian disruption, or micronutrient deficits?
6. Does GLA intake meaningfully alter prolactin or cortisol in modern human studies, or is this mostly historical hypothesis territory?

HIGHEST-VALUE NEXT EVIDENCE NEEDS
- Direct human refeeding studies after prolonged ketogenic adaptation.
- Direct human studies of postprandial somnolence with glucose or CGM data.
- Full-text access to primary fasting-prolactin and carb-thyroid studies.

NEW OPEN QUESTIONS AFTER ROUND 2
7. Is the dominant symptomatic pathway early orexin/serotonin-related sleepiness, later hyperinsulinemic crash, or a mix that differs by meal size and latency? [S013,S014]
8. How often does excessive postprandial sleepiness occur without measurable biochemical hypoglycemia in otherwise healthy adults? [S012,S014]
9. Is IL-1-mediated fatigue relevant only after large mixed meals or exercise-plus-meal stressors, or does it generalize to ordinary carbohydrate reintroduction? [S018]
10. Are large postprandial triglyceride excursions more important than glucose excursions for the fatigue component in some individuals, given GlycA/TG coupling and weak IL-6/glucose linkage? [S019]
11. Why do theoretically anti-inflammatory versus pro-inflammatory single meals often fail to differentially shift circulating cytokines? Is the issue marker choice, timing, statistical power, or true physiologic robustness? [S020]
12. Does meal timing alter symptom severity mainly by shifting peripheral glucose rhythms rather than by changing cortisol or subjective baseline sleepiness? [S015]
13. Is prolactin elevation in the target state actually causal, or does current evidence suggest the thyroid axis is the more consistent endocrine responder to fasting/carbohydrate deprivation? [S016]
14. Does oleic-acid-derived incretin signaling meaningfully alter sleepiness phenomenology, or is it merely a satiety/GLP-1 modifier without major sedative impact? [S017]
15. Are GLA/PGE1 claims functionally unsupported in modern human endocrine literature, such that the mechanism should remain permanently low-priority unless better evidence emerges?


ROUND 2 ADDITIONS
NEW OPEN QUESTIONS
7. Does the sleepiness begin within 30-60 minutes, or only after 2-5 hours? This timing strongly changes the ranking of orexin/serotonin/inflammatory versus reactive-hypoglycemia mechanisms.
8. Is carbohydrate reintroduced in the morning, afternoon, or evening? Round-2 evidence suggests evening carbohydrate loads may produce larger glucose/insulin excursions even in healthy people.
9. Does a small amount of carbohydrate trigger symptoms, or only large/refined/high-glycemic loads?
10. Are symptoms different with carbohydrate alone versus carbohydrate plus fat/protein?
11. Does continuous glucose monitoring show a high early peak, a rapid fall, a later nadir, or no clear glucose abnormality at all?
12. Would IL-1-related mechanisms still matter if CRP/IL-6 are normal? Round-2 evidence suggests yes.
13. Is prolactin reproducibly elevated on repeated standardized morning fasting draws, or could it be lab/context noise?
14. Does the specific fatty-acid blend alter subjective satiety or gastric emptying enough to change meal experience independently of glucose?

NEW ASSUMPTIONS TO TRACK
6. Acute and chronic low-carbohydrate states may produce overlapping but not identical mechanisms; round-2 evidence suggests both matter.
7. A normal postprandial cortisol time course would not exclude strong meal-timing effects, because glucose timing may shift without cortisol shifting.
8. Lack of biochemical hypoglycemia does not exclude cytokine- or orexin-mediated post-meal sleepiness.
