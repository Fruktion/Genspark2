TITLE
Mitigation and intervention strategies
PURPOSE
List evidence-based or mechanistically defensible strategies that could reduce post-carbohydrate sleepiness or normalize the upstream state, while clearly separating better-supported from speculative strategies.
STATUS
SUBSTANTIVE_DRAFT
SCOPE
Dietary composition, carbohydrate type/amount/timing, gradual reintroduction, monitoring, supplements/drugs with supporting rationale.
DEPENDENCIES
10_synthesis_of_mechanisms.txt, 13_confidence_assessment.txt.
KEY_QUESTIONS
What interventions logically target the leading mechanisms, and how strong is the supporting evidence?
EVIDENCE_TIER
Mixed clinical and mechanistic evidence.
SOURCE_IDS_USED
S001,S002,S004,S005,S006,S010,S011
OPEN_ISSUES
Few interventions directly studied in the exact phenotype; many recommendations remain mechanism-driven.
LAST_UPDATED
2026-04-03

PRINCIPLE
Mitigation should target the highest-confidence mechanisms first: exaggerated carbohydrate perturbation after low-carb adaptation, fasting/HPA overactivation, and timing-sensitive CNS sleep-promoting responses.

1. GRADUAL RATHER THAN ABRUPT CARBOHYDRATE REINTRODUCTION
Rationale:
If tissue glucose-handling machinery has adapted away from carbohydrate use [S001], abrupt large carbohydrate loads should be expected to produce a larger perturbation than gradual titration.
Evidence level:
Probable / mechanism-driven.
Why it may help:
- smaller excursions,
- less need for large insulin/incretin responses,
- more time for metabolic flexibility to return.

2. REDUCE CARBOHYDRATE DOSE PER MEAL AND LOWER GLYCEMIC LOAD
Rationale:
High-glycemic and larger carbohydrate exposures are more likely to provoke steep glucose-insulin dynamics and stronger CNS sleep-promoting responses [S010].
Evidence level:
Moderate.
Why it may help:
- smaller orexin suppression,
- less risk of reactive decline,
- less dramatic Trp/LNAA shift.

3. AVOID REFEEDING CARBOHYDRATE AFTER PROLONGED FASTS OR AT VULNERABLE CIRCADIAN TIMES
Rationale:
If cortisol and wakefulness are being supported by fasting-state stress physiology [S006], a large carbohydrate meal after a long fast may generate a bigger “state switch.”
Evidence level:
Probable.

4. VERIFY WHETHER TRUE REACTIVE HYPOGLYCEMIA EXISTS
Rationale:
CGM or symptom-timed glucose testing can distinguish true low glucose from rapid-fall or purely central/autonomic sleepiness.
Evidence level:
High practical value.

5. IMPROVE SLEEP AND REDUCE CHRONIC HPA LOAD
Rationale:
If hidden sleep pressure and cortisol-supported alertness are part of the syndrome, improving sleep quantity/quality may reduce the size of the post-meal crash.
Evidence level:
Probable.

6. PHARMACOLOGIC OR SUPPLEMENT OPTIONS
- For proven reactive hypoglycemia/prediabetic patterns, agents such as acarbose or metformin appear in adjacent literature [S004], but this depends on diagnosis and is not a generic recommendation.
- Omega-3 intake may modestly improve inflammatory/HPA context, but does not appear sufficient as a standalone fix [S011].

BOTTOM-LINE
Most evidence-based first-line interventions are behavioral/dietary: smaller carbohydrate doses, lower glycemic load, gradual reintroduction, better timing, and objective monitoring.
