TERMS
Ketosis: A metabolic state in which ketone bodies become a major circulating fuel due to very low carbohydrate availability.
Metabolic inflexibility: Reduced ability to switch efficiently between fat- and carbohydrate-based fuel oxidation.
GLUT4: Insulin-responsive glucose transporter, especially important in skeletal muscle and adipose tissue.
PDK4: Pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase 4; inhibits pyruvate dehydrogenase and shifts metabolism away from carbohydrate oxidation.
HPA axis: Hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis governing cortisol secretion and stress responses.
Prolactin: Pituitary hormone classically linked to lactation but also involved in metabolism and dopaminergic regulation.
Dopaminergic tone: Functional influence of dopamine signaling over target tissues and circuits.
Reactive hypoglycemia: Postprandial low glucose or symptomatic rapid glucose decline after eating.
Orexin/hypocretin: Hypothalamic neuropeptide system promoting wakefulness and integrating energy state with arousal.
Trp/LNAA ratio: Ratio of tryptophan to competing large neutral amino acids; influences brain tryptophan entry.
NF-kB: Major transcription factor involved in inflammatory signaling.
LPS: Lipopolysaccharide; endotoxin from gram-negative bacteria that can enter circulation postprandially.
TGR5 / FXR: Bile-acid-sensitive receptors with metabolic and anti-inflammatory roles.
Low T3 syndrome: Fall in triiodothyronine with normal/low-normal TSH and often increased reverse T3-like pattern during stress or underfeeding states.
OEA: Oleoylethanolamide, a lipid-derived satiety mediator related to oleic-acid metabolism.
GPR119: Receptor involved in incretin and metabolic signaling responsive to lipid-derived ligands.
