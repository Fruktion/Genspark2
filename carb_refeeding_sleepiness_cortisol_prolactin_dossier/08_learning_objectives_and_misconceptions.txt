LEARNING OBJECTIVES
1. Understand that post-carbohydrate sleepiness in this state is likely multi-causal, not a single insulin-only phenomenon.
2. Understand that strict low-carbohydrate adaptation can impair measured carbohydrate tolerance without necessarily implying pathology in all contexts [S001,S002].
3. Understand that elevated fasting cortisol is expected in fasting/energy-deficit states more often than elevated prolactin is [S006 vs S007].
4. Understand that prolactin’s role is probably modulatory through dopamine/arousal/metabolic pathways rather than the sole trigger of carbohydrate-induced sleepiness [S003].
5. Understand that inflammatory and endotoxin pathways may amplify fatigue after meals, especially high-fat/high-carbohydrate meals [S005].
6. Understand that serotonin/melatonin and orexin pathways offer a central nervous system bridge between carbohydrate ingestion and sleepiness [S010].

MISCONCEPTIONS TO AVOID
- “Food coma” is just blood going to the stomach.
  This is oversimplified and poorly explanatory.
- If ketosis lowers fasting glucose, it must improve all glucose handling.
  Human evidence contradicts that assumption [S001,S002].
- Elevated prolactin always means the same thing physiologically.
  Effects are context-dependent and can reflect multiple upstream processes [S003].
- Reactive hypoglycemia is the only explanation for postprandial sleepiness.
  Orexin, serotonin, autonomic, and inflammatory pathways also matter [S004,S010].
- Omega-3 intake necessarily prevents all inflammatory or stress-related meal responses.
  Evidence supports modulation, not total protection [S005,S011].
