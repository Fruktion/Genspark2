TOP-LINE ASSESSMENT
Prolactin is probably not the single primary explanation for carbohydrate-triggered sleepiness, but it is a credible amplifier. The best current evidence supports a general framework in which high prolactin and reduced dopaminergic tone are associated with lower arousal, altered appetite/reward regulation, and worse metabolic profiles [S003]. What is less well established is whether fasting reliably raises basal prolactin in a simple way. The currently opened primary fasting study more strongly suggests altered dopaminergic control of prolactin responsiveness than a universal fasting-induced prolactin rise [S007]. Thus, the safest current stance is: prolactin/dopamine dysregulation is a probable contributor, but weaker than the metabolic-refeeding and HPA-axis mechanisms.

1. PROLACTIN PHYSIOLOGY MOST RELEVANT TO THIS DOSSIER
Prolactin is tonically inhibited by hypothalamic dopamine via D2 receptor signaling. It is therefore unusual among pituitary hormones in being under strong inhibitory rather than stimulatory hypothalamic control [S003]. When prolactin rises, it can feed back into dopaminergic systems, but chronic high-prolactin states can also be associated with altered or refractory central dopaminergic tone [S003].

Why this matters here: dopamine is a major wakefulness, motivation, and salience neurotransmitter. If prolactin is elevated and dopaminergic tone is reduced, the person’s baseline arousal reserve may already be compromised. In that state, any meal-induced sleep-promoting mechanisms—orexin suppression, serotonin bias, parasympathetic shift—could feel much more dramatic.

2. DOES FASTING ELEVATE PROLACTIN?
This is more complicated than the cortisol story. The currently opened fasting literature does not justify a strong universal statement that fasting straightforwardly raises basal prolactin in all humans.
- S007 found that in obese men, prolactin responsiveness to metoclopramide was blunted in the fed state and “normalized” after a 56-hour fast, suggesting fasting altered dopaminergic control of lactotroph function [S007].
- Another opened abstract (PMID 6404923, included conceptually in the source-search process but not assigned a new source ID here) reported that prolonged fasting affected thyrotropin responsiveness while prolactin responses to TRH/cimetidine were not simply increased, again arguing against an overly simple “fasting raises PRL” rule.

Therefore, if the user reports elevated fasting prolactin, the finding should be taken seriously, but the literature currently in hand suggests that its interpretation is state- and context-dependent. Possible meanings include:
- altered hypothalamic dopaminergic restraint,
- stress-related neuroendocrine dysregulation,
- measurement timing/context effect,
- or a genuine fasting-associated shift in this individual.

3. HOW ELEVATED PROLACTIN COULD REDUCE AROUSAL
S003 provides the main conceptual support. The review links hyperprolactinemia to disruption of CNS dopaminergic tone, hyperphagia, weight gain, and insulin-resistance-related changes [S003]. Although the review is not specifically about alertness, reduced dopaminergic tone is highly relevant to wakefulness and motivation. The resulting picture is that elevated prolactin can create a lower-arousal baseline, even before meals.

This would not by itself explain the carbohydrate specificity of the symptom, but it can explain why the person crashes more easily after a carbohydrate-triggered postprandial shift.

4. PROLACTIN AND METABOLIC FUNCTION
S003 emphasizes that prolactin has complex metabolic actions on adipose tissue, pancreas, liver, and appetite circuitry [S003]. High pathological prolactin states are associated with worse insulin resistance, dyslipidemia, and obesity, while a “goldilocks zone” may exist in which normal-high prolactin is metabolically neutral or beneficial [S003].

For this dossier, two takeaways matter:
- The meaning of “elevated prolactin” depends on magnitude and context.
- Prolactin can interact with glucose handling and appetite/arousal circuitry, making it reasonable to treat as a contributor to postprandial symptoms.

5. DOPAMINE SUPPRESSION AND POSTPRANDIAL SLEEPINESS
If prolactin elevation reflects reduced hypothalamic dopaminergic restraint, then postprandial sleepiness could become worse for at least three reasons:
1. Lower baseline motivational/arousal tone.
2. Lower resistance to meal-induced serotonin/orexin-related sleep pressure.
3. Greater susceptibility to the subjective “shut-down” or reduced reward-drive feeling that some people describe after meals.

This is conceptually coherent but still not directly proven in the exact phenotype.

6. PROLACTIN-CORTISOL INTERACTION
The prompt asks for mutual amplification. The currently opened evidence base is not strong enough to claim a well-established, direct cortisol-prolactin mutual amplification loop in this exact setting. What can be said safely is that both hormones are stress-responsive in some contexts, both can interact with hypothalamic regulation, and both can alter metabolic state. They may co-occur during neuroendocrine strain, but whether one is driving the other in this scenario remains uncertain. This belongs in the “probable co-regulation / possible mutual reinforcement” category rather than “established loop.”

7. GLA, PGE1, AND HORROBIN-TYPE HYPOTHESES
This is the weakest portion of the requested mechanism set. Historical literature and review-level materials suggest that GLA, through conversion to dihomo-gamma-linolenic acid and then PGE1-related pathways, has been hypothesized to modulate prolactin actions and endocrine symptoms. But the available material is old, heterogeneous, and sometimes internally contradictory. Search-opened records also show older findings that PGE1 can stimulate prolactin release in some experimental settings, which complicates simplistic claims that PGE1 necessarily antagonizes prolactin effects.

Therefore:
- It is reasonable to mention GLA/PGE1 as a speculative modifier.
- It is not reasonable, based on current opened evidence, to treat GLA as a major established cause of elevated prolactin or of the reported sleepiness.

8. RED TEAM / HOW THIS COULD BE WRONG
- Elevated prolactin may be epiphenomenal rather than causal.
- Dopaminergic tone may be reduced for reasons unrelated to prolactin.
- Fasting prolactin elevation may not be reproducible if remeasured under standardized conditions.
- The symptom could remain unchanged even if prolactin were normalized, implying it is not a primary driver.
- GLA may have little or no meaningful endocrine effect in this context.

[ROUND 2 ADDITION]
9. STRONGER BOUNDARY EVIDENCE AGAINST A SIMPLE FASTING-PROLACTIN MODEL
Round-2 expansion added S016, which is more constraining than supportive for strong prolactin claims. In healthy volunteers, 56-hour fasting markedly suppressed TSH responsiveness, but TRH- and cimetidine-induced prolactin responses were broadly unaffected [S016]. This means the current dossier has better direct support for fasting sensitivity of the thyroid axis than for a generalized fasting-induced prolactin elevation model.

That does not make the reported elevated fasting prolactin irrelevant. It does mean the safest interpretation is now narrower:
- elevated prolactin may still act as an amplifier of reduced arousal reserve,
- but fasting per se is not strongly established as a universal causal explanation for that elevation,
- and prolactin evidence is weaker than the metabolic-refeeding and CNS pathways.

[ROUND 2 ADDITION]
10. CONSEQUENCE FOR DOPAMINE-AROUSAL FRAMING
The most defensible role for the prolactin/dopamine axis after round 2 is an amplifier model rather than a central-driver model. S003 still supports the broader biology of prolactin-dopamine-metabolism interaction [S003], and S007 supports altered dopaminergic regulation in a fasting context [S007]. But S016 prevents overreach by showing that prolonged fasting does not simply amplify prolactin responsiveness across the board [S016].

BOTTOM-LINE JUDGMENT
Established:
- Prolactin is tightly linked to dopaminergic control and broader metabolic regulation [S003].
Probable:
- Elevated prolactin / reduced dopaminergic tone can lower arousal reserve and amplify postprandial sleepiness.
Uncertain:
- Whether fasting is the direct reason prolactin is elevated in this case.
Low confidence / speculative:
- Strong GLA/PGE1-centered explanations for the phenotype.
Boundary reminder:
- Direct human fasting evidence currently weighs more strongly for thyroid-axis changes than for a universal prolactin-rise model [S016].


[ROUND 2 ADDITION]
9. NEW DIRECT HUMAN NULL EVIDENCE AGAINST A STRONG GLA-LOWERS-PROLACTIN CLAIM
S019 is highly useful because it finally adds a modern randomized human full-text source rather than relying only on older hypotheses. In women with PMS treated for six months with an essential-fatty-acid preparation containing gamma-linolenic acid, linoleic acid, oleic acid, and vitamin E, symptoms improved but serum prolactin did not significantly change [S019]. This directly weakens any simplistic claim that GLA-containing preparations reliably lower circulating prolactin in humans.

10. HOW S019 CHANGES THE GLA INTERPRETATION
The safest interpretation after S019 is:
- GLA-containing interventions may alter symptoms in some contexts.
- The effect is not convincingly mediated by lowering basal serum prolactin.
- If any prolactin-related effect exists, it is more likely via altered tissue sensitivity or downstream signaling than via robust serum-level reduction [S019].
This moves GLA-centered endocrine claims from “possible contributor” toward “low-confidence modifier only.”

11. NEW FASTING-PROLACTIN CONTRADICTION FROM S018
S018 adds another important primary-human contradiction. In healthy volunteers, a 56-hour fast markedly suppressed TSH responsiveness but did not restore or globally increase prolactin responses in a way that would support a simple “fasting raises prolactin” model. Metoclopramide altered some prolactin responses, but the broader picture remained one of selective pituitary adaptation rather than a uniform prolactin increase [S018].

12. ROUND-2 JUDGMENT UPDATE
With S018 and S019 added, prolactin remains plausible as an arousal amplifier, but fasting-induced hyperprolactinemia is now even less convincing as a default explanation. The dossier should therefore emphasize:
- prolactin elevation in the user may be real,
- but fasting does not appear to raise prolactin in a simple, reliable, one-direction way,
- and GLA-containing fats do not have good direct evidence for lowering circulating prolactin.
