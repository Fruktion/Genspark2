TOPIC
Physiological causes of excessive sleepiness after reintroducing carbohydrates during caloric deficit with complete carbohydrate restriction, high protein intake (~2 g/kg), high fat intake (~1 g/kg from EPA+DHA, GLA, and oleic acid), and elevated fasting cortisol/prolactin.

CURRENT WORKING MODEL
The most defensible current model is multi-causal rather than single-cause. The leading primary mechanisms are: (1) low-carbohydrate adaptation causing transient peripheral glucose intolerance and reduced muscle glucose disposal capacity on refeeding; (2) HPA-axis activation from fasting/caloric deficit/carb restriction; (3) postprandial glycemic overshoot and sometimes reactive glycemic decline with orexin suppression/counter-regulatory instability; (4) altered arousal tone from elevated prolactin and reduced dopaminergic tone; and (5) postprandial inflammatory and autonomic signals that can amplify fatigue. Fatty-acid composition likely modifies these processes, but the exact combination-specific effect of EPA+DHA + GLA + oleic acid is supported mainly by adjacent rather than direct evidence.

MOST IMPORTANT CURRENT FINDINGS
1. Human randomized evidence supports a state of reduced carbohydrate tolerance after weeks of ketogenic restriction, with early increased PDK4 and later decreased skeletal-muscle GLUT4, insulin receptor, and AMPK. This makes reduced peripheral glucose disposal a primary-candidate mechanism for exaggerated post-carb symptoms.
2. Human fasting/time-restricted-feeding literature supports elevated cortisol output and altered cortisol timing during fasting states, plausibly increasing baseline hyperarousal and making carbohydrate ingestion feel relatively sedating as cortisol falls and parasympathetic/postprandial processes dominate.
3. Postprandial sleepiness is mechanistically plausible through combined serotonin-melatonin, orexin suppression, vagal activation, glycemic excursion, and inflammatory signaling rather than any single "blood-flow redistribution" explanation.
4. Elevated prolactin can coexist with reduced dopaminergic tone and lower arousal/motivation, but direct proof that fasting-induced prolactin elevation is the main driver in this phenotype is weaker than the metabolic-refeeding mechanism.
5. EPA/DHA likely dampen inflammatory/TLR4/NF-kB signaling; this may blunt some postprandial inflammatory consequences, but does not eliminate glucose-tolerance or HPA-axis mechanisms.
6. GLA/PGE1 claims around prolactin are historically interesting but more weakly evidenced and internally conflicted than the metabolic/HPA mechanisms.

MAJOR UNCERTAINTIES
1. Whether this phenotype reflects true reactive hypoglycemia, rapid glucose decline without biochemical hypoglycemia, or predominantly central/autonomic postprandial somnolence.
2. The extent to which elevated prolactin is a stable fasting adaptation versus a context-specific or measurement-timing artifact.
3. Whether the specific high-dose EPA+DHA/GLA/oleic-acid combination meaningfully alters incretin response, cerebral perfusion, or cortisol-prolactin coupling in humans.
4. How much caloric deficit magnitude, sleep debt, and meal timing explain variance across individuals.
