WHY THIS SECTION IS ADJACENT, NOT DIRECT
Stress manipulations co-activate catecholamines, appraisal, autonomic arousal, and glucocorticoids. Therefore endogenous stress evidence cannot be read as pure cortisol evidence. It is valuable mainly for boundary conditions.

KEY EVIDENCE
1) Arnsten 2009 (S011)
- Acute stress rapidly impairs PFC-mediated cognition, especially working memory and attention.
- Noradrenaline and dopamine each show inverted-U relationships in the PFC.
- Glucocorticoids can worsen PFC function and interact with catecholamine systems, but are not acting alone.
- The paper explicitly undermines any single linear cortisol-equivalent rule for PFC function.
Classification: MECHANISTIC_BOUNDARY_SOURCE

2) Shields et al. 2016 meta-analytic review (S013)
- Acute stress impairs working memory and cognitive flexibility overall, with more nuanced effects on inhibition.
- This domain-splitting supports the dossier rule to separate executive subdomains instead of speaking of “cognition” globally.
Classification: TASK_TAXONOMY_AND_BOUNDARY_SOURCE

3) Kalafatakis et al. 2018 pattern study (S022)
- Even under controlled exogenous replacement, emotional and working-memory outcomes depend on temporal glucocorticoid pattern.
- This strengthens the broader point that endogenous stress curves and exogenous steroid dosing are not simply interconvertible.
Classification: BRIDGE_SOURCE

BOUNDARY CONDITIONS DERIVED FROM ADJACENT LITERATURE
- Working memory is especially sensitive to stress-related disruption.
- Inhibition can behave differently from working memory or flexibility.
- Emotional context matters.
- Cortisol effects are shaped by concurrent catecholaminergic state and time course.

WHAT THIS SECTION DOES NOT LICENSE
- It does not justify converting stress-induced salivary cortisol changes into dexamethasone-equivalent cognitive predictions.
- It does not prove that administering glucocorticoids alone reproduces whole-stress PFC outcomes.

PROVISIONAL TAKEAWAY
Adjacent stress literature supports an inverted-U, state-dependent, and task-specific framework. It strengthens the case against any single dexamethasone-to-cortisol conversion for PFC function but should not be mistaken for direct dexamethasone evidence.
