TITLE: Disconfirming evidence and red-team kill shots
PURPOSE: Search for and log evidence that could overturn tempting but weak conclusions.
STATUS: IN_PROGRESS
SCOPE: Four target conclusions (A-D) identified as especially tempting and especially vulnerable to overstatement.
DEPENDENCIES: 04_source_register.txt; 33_contradictions_null_results_task_dependence_and_boundary_conditions.txt
KEY_QUESTIONS: What evidence directly undermines each tempting conclusion; what would falsify current working models.
EVIDENCE_TIER: RED_TEAM
SOURCE_IDS_USED: S001; S005; S014; S017; S021; S022; S023; S024; S007; S015
OPEN_ISSUES: More direct low-dose dexamethasone studies could further strengthen or overturn conclusion A.
LAST_UPDATED: 2026-04-01

TARGET CONCLUSION A
Tempting claim: “0.5 mg oral dexamethasone probably corresponds to some meaningful cortisol-equivalent blood level for PFC function.”
Kill shots:
- S021: post-dexamethasone cortisol is a suppression-test outcome confounded by dex exposure, binding proteins, and assay type; not a cognitive-equivalence readout.
- S023: dexamethasone has poor MR substitution and poor BBB penetration relative to endogenous glucocorticoids.
- S022: same total glucocorticoid dose can yield different cognition depending on rhythm, so any single blood-level mapping is underdetermined.
Red-team verdict: CURRENTLY OVERTURNED / NOT JUSTIFIED.

TARGET CONCLUSION B
Tempting claim: “Glucocorticoids generally improve cognition if the dose is low or physiological.”
Kill shots:
- S005: higher-vs-lower hydrocortisone replacement dose in SAI produced no cognitive benefit.
- S014: large salivary cortisol elevation in healthy elderly produced null effects.
- S002: acute 10 mg hydrocortisone impaired working-memory/interference performance in healthy adults.
Red-team verdict: OVERTURNED AS A GENERAL RULE.

TARGET CONCLUSION C
Tempting claim: “Dexamethasone and hydrocortisone can be treated as interchangeable glucocorticoid tools for cognition.”
Kill shots:
- S023: dexamethasone acts mainly via pituitary feedback, is BBB-limited, and does not recreate MR-rich brain states.
- S001: direct dexamethasone arm in healthy volunteers was null on WM/flexibility despite endocrine change.
- S003/S022: hydrocortisone studies show timing/pattern-sensitive direct cognitive effects not inferable from dexamethasone suppression logic.
Red-team verdict: OVERTURNED.

TARGET CONCLUSION D
Tempting claim: “Replacement benefits in adrenal insufficiency show glucocorticoids are nootropics.”
Kill shots:
- S007: animal rescue evidence is normalization after deficiency, not enhancement above baseline.
- S024: physiologic-pattern replacement improved some affect/fatigue/emotional-processing measures but primary cognitive outcome and N-back were null.
- S017: some AI cohorts show only subtle deficits rather than dramatic reversible cognitive failure.
Red-team verdict: OVERTURNED.

FALSIFIER WATCHLIST
Evidence that would meaningfully shift the dossier later:
1) A direct randomized healthy-human study showing 0.5 mg oral dexamethasone improves a validated PFC task with endocrine/PK context.
2) A study demonstrating dexamethasone brain receptor occupancy or central exposure equivalent to hydrocortisone/cortisol under matched conditions.
3) Robust adrenal-insufficiency trials showing broad objective executive-function normalization with physiologic replacement patterns.
4) Repeated animal data showing glucocorticoid administration enhances intact-animal PFC tasks above baseline, not merely rescues deficiency.

PROVISIONAL TAKEAWAY
The strongest red-team result of this round is that the dossier’s most tempting shortcut—a single dex-to-cortisol conversion for PFC function—fails simultaneously at DST logic, receptor logic, BBB logic, and rhythm logic.
