CONTRADICTION SET 1: “More glucocorticoid exposure improves cognition.”
- Contradicted by S002: 10 mg hydrocortisone impaired working-memory/interference performance in healthy adults under negative interference.
- Contradicted by S015: chronic hydrocortisone impaired frontal-lobe-sensitive tasks in healthy men.
- Contradicted by S014: large salivary cortisol elevations in healthy elderly produced no significant cognitive effect.
- Contradicted by S005: higher replacement dose in secondary adrenal insufficiency did not improve cognition.
Status: DISCONFIRMED_AS_GENERAL_RULE

CONTRADICTION SET 2: “More glucocorticoid exposure impairs cognition.”
- Contradicted by S003: slow-timed hydrocortisone improved working-memory-related performance and DLPFC activation in healthy young men.
- Contradicted by S020: acute hydrocortisone improved response inhibition in healthy controls.
- Contradicted/qualified by S024 and S022: more physiological glucocorticoid patterning improved some functions even when total dose was held constant.
Status: DISCONFIRMED_AS_GENERAL_RULE

CONTRADICTION SET 3: “Dexamethasone suppression results can be read as cortisol-equivalent cognitive exposure.”
- Contradicted by S021: post-DST cortisol depends on dexamethasone adequacy, binding proteins, and assay type; false positives occur.
- Contradicted by S023: dexamethasone differs from cortisol in MR affinity and BBB penetration.
Status: DISCONFIRMED

CONTRADICTION SET 4: “Replacement normalization implies enhancement in healthy people.”
- Contradicted by S007: corticosterone restored ADX-induced rat working-memory deficits to baseline rather than above baseline.
- Contradicted by human AI literature S024/S005/S006: partial normalization or null results in deficiency states do not imply general enhancement.
Status: DISCONFIRMED

TASK-DEPENDENCE LOG
- Working memory can worsen (S002), improve with delayed timing (S003), or remain unchanged (S014, S024).
- Cognitive flexibility/WM can be null under dexamethasone despite endocrine change (S001).
- Response inhibition may improve acutely in healthy controls (S020), which should not be generalized to all executive function.
- Emotional ambiguity and affect may improve under more physiological replacement while N-back remains null (S024).

POPULATION-DEPENDENCE LOG
- Healthy young men: timing-sensitive enhancement seen with hydrocortisone (S003).
- Healthy elderly: null despite large cortisol elevation (S014).
- MDD patients: hydrocortisone effects can disappear relative to healthy controls (S002, S020).
- Adrenal-insufficiency patients: mixed affect/fatigue improvements but weak objective cognitive gains (S024).

TEMPORAL / PATTERN-DEPENDENCE LOG
- Rapid versus slow hydrocortisone effects differ (S003).
- Same total daily hydrocortisone dose delivered in different rhythms gives different working-memory outcomes (S022).
- Acute versus chronic hydrocortisone do not behave alike (S003 vs S015).

BOUNDARY CONDITIONS FOR INTERPRETATION
1) Do not collapse executive functions into one outcome.
2) Do not infer cognition from endocrine suppression.
3) Do not generalize across healthy, deficient, depressed, or endocrine-disease populations.
4) Do not equate acute timing effects with chronic replacement effects.
5) Do not treat null primary outcomes as proof of no domain-specific benefit or harm.

PROVISIONAL TAKEAWAY
The contradiction pattern is not noise; it is the signal. Glucocorticoid effects on cognition are highly conditional on task, timing, population, deficiency state, and measurement domain.
