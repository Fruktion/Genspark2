DIRECT STUDIES

Study: S004
Citation: Harbeck et al. 2009 pilot nocturnal cortisol replacement study
Species: Human
Sex: Mixed
Health state / model: Primary or secondary adrenal insufficiency
Endogenous glucocorticoid context: Cortisol-deficient state on replacement therapy
Intervention compound: Intravenous hydrocortisone infusion overnight
Dose: Overnight infusion from midnight to 8 am; exact mg schedule not fully extracted in current round
Route: IV infusion
Timing relative to testing: Tested after infusion versus during usual oral therapy condition
Acute vs chronic: Short-term replacement-pattern manipulation
Comparator: Usual oral glucocorticoid therapy
Whether cortisol measured: Yes
Assay type: Plasma cortisol in study context; abstract-only extraction
Cognitive task: Neurocognitive battery including concentration and memory tasks
PFC relevance: Partial
Direction of effect: Mostly null; one digit-symbol outcome favored oral condition; higher cortisol correlated with poorer short-term memory
Classification: Predominantly null, not enhancement
Major confounders: Small pilot; abstract only; heterogeneous population
Source-access grade: ABSTRACT_ONLY
Evidence-strength note: Important because a more physiological night-time replacement did not produce broad cognitive gains.

Study: S005
Citation: Buning et al. 2015 randomized controlled trial in secondary adrenal insufficiency
Species: Human
Sex: Mixed
Health state / model: Secondary adrenal insufficiency
Endogenous glucocorticoid context: Chronic replacement state
Intervention compound: Hydrocortisone low vs high dose
Dose: Low 0.2-0.3 mg/kg/day versus high 0.4-0.6 mg/kg/day
Route: Oral replacement regimen
Timing relative to testing: After 10 weeks on each regimen
Acute vs chronic: Chronic crossover dosing comparison
Comparator: Lower-dose versus higher-dose physiological replacement
Whether cortisol measured: Yes
Assay type: Systemic cortisol measured 1 h after dose; abstract-level extraction
Cognitive task: Memory, attention, executive function, social cognition battery
PFC relevance: Yes for executive/attention components
Direction of effect: No significant cognitive differences despite higher systemic cortisol in higher-dose condition
Classification: Null dose-response within replacement range
Major confounders: Abstract only; may not address pulsatility/rhythm
Source-access grade: ABSTRACT_ONLY
Evidence-strength note: Strongly argues against assuming that more replacement cortisol yields better cognition.

Study: S024
Citation: Russell et al. 2024 PULSES trial
Species: Human
Sex: Mixed, mostly female; fMRI subset restricted in analysis for homogeneity
Health state / model: Primary adrenal insufficiency (mostly Addison disease, some CAH)
Endogenous glucocorticoid context: Cortisol-deficient clinical population receiving replacement
Intervention compound: Usual total daily hydrocortisone delivered as pulsatile subcutaneous infusion versus standard oral therapy
Dose: Usual total daily dose 20-30 mg/day
Route: Pulsatile subcutaneous pump versus oral tablets
Timing relative to testing: After 6 weeks on each treatment arm in randomized crossover design
Acute vs chronic: Chronic short-term crossover pattern manipulation
Comparator: Standard three-times-daily oral hydrocortisone
Whether cortisol measured: Yes
Assay type: 24 h blood sampling for cortisol and ACTH in a subset; other outcomes included questionnaires, ETB, N-back, and fMRI
Cognitive task: FERT, ECAT, FDOT, EREC, N-back, fMRI emotional processing
PFC relevance: Partial; emotional-processing networks plus working memory
Direction of effect: Primary FERT null; ECAT/emotional-ambiguity signals improved on pulsatile; N-back null; subjective fatigue and positive affect improved; ease of awakening improved
Classification: Mixed, consistent with partial normalization of affect/fatigue/emotional processing rather than general cognitive enhancement
Major confounders: Small sample; higher cortisol AUC on pulsatile despite matched nominal dose; feasibility duration only 6 weeks; adrenal-insufficient population only
Source-access grade: FULL_TEXT_OPENED
Evidence-strength note: High-value replacement-context study showing that better physiology can improve some clinically relevant domains without producing broad objective cognitive enhancement.

INTERIM PATTERN SUMMARY
- Replacement studies do not support a general rule that more glucocorticoid replacement improves cognition.
- Higher replacement dose can be null (S005).
- More physiological timing/pattern can yield mixed benefits in fatigue, affect, or emotional ambiguity while leaving primary cognitive endpoints null (S004, S024).
- Long-term treated patients may still show mild or subtle deficits relative to controls (S006, S017).
- Best current interpretation is normalization-context dependence, not enhancement.

PROVISIONAL INFERENCE (NOT A SOURCE)
Human replacement evidence supports, at most, context-specific partial normalization in deficiency states. It does not justify a general cognition-enhancement conclusion for glucocorticoids.
