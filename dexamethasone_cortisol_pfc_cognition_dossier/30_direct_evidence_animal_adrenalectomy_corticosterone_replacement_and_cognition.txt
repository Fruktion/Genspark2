DIRECT STUDIES

Study: S007
Citation: Mizoguchi et al. 2004 J Neurosci
Species: Rat (Wistar)
Sex: Male
Health state / model: Adrenalectomized rats (ADX) versus sham; glucocorticoid-deficient state induced surgically
Endogenous glucocorticoid context: Endogenous corticosterone suppressed/removed by bilateral adrenalectomy
Intervention compound: Corticosterone replacement; plus mechanistic intra-PFC D1 agonist rescue experiments
Dose: 100 mg 60-day release corticosterone pellet + nocturnal drinking corticosterone 50 microg/ml from 7 PM to 7 AM
Route: Subcutaneous pellet + drinking water; separate intra-PFC SKF81297 infusions for mechanistic rescue
Timing relative to testing: Testing 21 days after surgery/replacement initiation
Acute vs chronic: Chronic deficiency/replacement model
Comparator: Sham surgery + placebo pellet; ADX + placebo pellet; ADX + corticosterone replacement
Whether corticosterone measured: Yes
Assay type: Plasma corticosterone radioimmunoassay (RIA)
Cognitive task: Delayed nonmatch-to-sample T-maze task with 0, 10, 30, 60 s delays
Plausibly PFC-dependent: Yes; authors explicitly frame it as PFC-sensitive working memory, especially at longer delays
Direction of effect: ADX impaired working memory at 30 and 60 s delays; corticosterone replacement prevented/reversed this impairment
Normalization / enhancement / impairment / mixed / null: Normalization/rescue of deficiency-induced impairment, not enhancement above sham baseline
Major confounders: Male rats only; corticosterone not human cortisol; task is rodent analog of working memory, not human executive testing; replacement aims to reproduce physiological plasma levels rather than exceed them
Source-access grade: FULL_TEXT_OPENED
Evidence-strength note: High-value causal animal study directly showing that endogenous glucocorticoids are necessary for maintaining PFC-like working memory in deficient rats.

Species: Rat (Wistar)
Sex: Male
Health state / model: ADX with or without corticosterone replacement
Endogenous glucocorticoid context: Glucocorticoid suppression by adrenalectomy
Intervention compound: Corticosterone replacement
Dose: Not fully extracted in current round beyond abstract context as follow-up to prior ADX paradigm
Route: Not fully extracted in current round

WHAT THE ANIMAL EVIDENCE SUPPORTS
- Removing endogenous corticosterone can impair rodent PFC-like working memory.
- Replacing corticosterone to physiological levels can normalize this deficit.
- This is best interpreted as maintenance/restoration of a necessary hormonal context, not generic enhancement beyond intact baseline.

WHAT IT DOES NOT SUPPORT
- It does not show that giving glucocorticoids to already intact healthy animals improves cognition above baseline.
- It does not justify direct conversion from rodent corticosterone replacement to human cortisol or dexamethasone use.
- It does not justify mapping dexamethasone serum suppression effects onto corticosterone replacement effects.

PROVISIONAL INFERENCE (NOT A SOURCE)
Animal replacement data support a necessity/normalization model in deficient states more than an enhancement model in intact organisms.
