CORE HPA RHYTHM POINTS
- Physiological cortisol is not a flat concentration. It follows a circadian pattern with an overnight rise, awakening peak, daytime decline, and superimposed ultradian pulses (S022, S025).
- The ultradian rhythm is not cosmetic; it changes tissue and brain responses to the same total hormone dose (S022).
- In adrenal insufficiency, conventional oral replacement creates dissonant circulating cortisol oscillations rather than normal physiology (S025).

WHY RHYTHM MATTERS FOR COGNITION
- In a metyrapone block-and-replace study, the same 20 mg/day hydrocortisone dose administered in different temporal patterns produced different working-memory and emotional-processing outcomes (S022).
- Pulsatile replacement preserved higher-load n-back performance better than continuous or standard oral patterns (S022).
- Therefore cognitive interpretation requires attention to timing/pattern, not just nominal dose or average concentration.

DST ASSAY LIMITS
- Genere et al. used 1 mg dexamethasone at 11 pm with morning blood sampling and showed that interpretation depends on measured dexamethasone concentration, total cortisol, and free cortisol (S021).
- Total cortisol was measured by immunoassay, while dexamethasone and free cortisol were measured by LC-MS/MS-based methods (S021).
- Oral contraceptives and altered cortisol-binding globulin can create false-positive total-cortisol DST results without corresponding free-cortisol abnormality (S021).
- Therefore assay modality and protein-binding state materially affect interpretation.

CONCRETE NUMERICAL LIMITS FROM OPENED SOURCE
- Adequate dexamethasone concentrations were present in 97.6% of volunteers and 96.3% of patients in the Genere study (S021).
- All false-positive total-cortisol results occurred in the post-DST total cortisol range 1.8-5 mcg/dL, with OCP use being the main associated factor (S021).
- Implication: even within endocrine diagnosis, post-dex cortisol requires contextual interpretation.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE DOSSIER QUESTION
1) A single serum cortisol number is not a full descriptor of glucocorticoid signaling.
2) Post-dexamethasone cortisol values are feedback-test outputs, not cortisol-equivalent CNS exposures.
3) Replacement studies that improve rhythm may alter cognition even without changing nominal daily dose.
4) Conversely, large cortisol elevations may fail to alter cognition if timing, task, age, or receptor context differ (cross-reference S014, S003).

CLAIM STATUS GRID
- “Circadian and ultradian features matter to cognition” -> SUPPORTED [S022, S025]
- “Conventional oral replacement reproduces natural physiology” -> DISCONFIRMED [S025]
- “DST cortisol is straightforwardly interpretable without assay/binding context” -> DISCONFIRMED [S021]
- “Average dose or concentration alone can define PFC-equivalent effect” -> NOT SUPPORTED [S021, S022, S025]

TRACEABILITY NOTES
Inference: linking rhythm findings to PFC conclusions is a synthesis from S022 plus direct PFC-task literature, not a direct quote.
Unresolved: whether any specific low dexamethasone dose can mimic a physiologic cognitive rhythm remains unanswered.

PROVISIONAL TAKEAWAY
For cognition/PFC questions, glucocorticoid timing, pulsatility, assay context, and feedback physiology are first-order variables, not nuisances. Any framework that ignores them is too crude for justified dexamethasone-to-cortisol translation.
