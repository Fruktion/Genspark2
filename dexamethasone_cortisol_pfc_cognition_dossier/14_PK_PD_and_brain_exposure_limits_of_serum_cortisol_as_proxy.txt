WHY SERUM IS NOT THE BRAIN
1) BBB transport
- Dexamethasone entry into brain is restricted by P-glycoprotein, limiting direct translation from blood levels to central receptor exposure (S008, S023 cross-reference).

2) Protein binding
- Total cortisol depends strongly on cortisol-binding globulin and albumin states; free cortisol can diverge materially from total cortisol in DST interpretation (S021).

3) Temporal structure
- Same daily dose can generate different brain/cognitive effects when delivered with different pulsatile patterns (S022).

4) Local metabolism and tissue decoding
- Review literature in ACI notes tissue-specific handling and response variability, including factors not captured by one serum sample (S025).

IMPLICATION FOR DEXAMETHASONE QUESTION
If serum total cortisol after dexamethasone is already vulnerable to binding and assay confounds, and dexamethasone itself has restricted brain entry, then post-dex blood values cannot serve as a reliable surrogate for PFC glucocorticoid state.

WHAT SERUM MEASURES STILL CAN DO
- Confirm broad endocrine directionality (e.g., hydrocortisone elevation, dexamethasone suppression, inadequate dex exposure suspicion).
- Support pharmacologic manipulation checks in intervention studies.
They cannot, on current evidence, uniquely specify receptor balance or cognitive consequence.

CLAIM STATUS
- “Serum cortisol is useful as an endocrine manipulation check” -> SUPPORTED [S003, S021]
- “Serum/post-dex cortisol is a sufficient proxy for brain/PFC exposure” -> NOT SUPPORTED [S008, S021, S022]
- “A blood-level conversion can stand in for cognitive equivalence” -> NOT SUPPORTED [S021, S022, S025]

PROVISIONAL TAKEAWAY
Serum values are necessary but not sufficient. For this dossier’s PFC question, blood levels are too coarse to justify a single dexamethasone-to-cortisol cognitive translation.
