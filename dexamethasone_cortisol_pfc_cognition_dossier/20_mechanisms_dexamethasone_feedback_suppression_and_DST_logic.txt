DST LOGIC IN ONE LINE
The dexamethasone suppression test measures how the HPA axis suppresses endogenous cortisol after exogenous dexamethasone; it does not measure a cortisol-like cognitive or brain-substitution effect.

MECHANISTIC CHAIN
1) Dexamethasone is given exogenously.
2) It acts strongly at feedback sites, especially pituitary corticotrophs, suppressing ACTH and therefore endogenous cortisol production (S023; S009).
3) Morning cortisol after the test is the residual endogenous cortisol outcome under this feedback challenge (S021).
4) That value depends on dexamethasone exposure, assay choice, and binding context (S021).

WHY POST-DEX CORTISOL IS NOT A CORTISOL-EQUIVALENT EFFECT
- The measured cortisol is what remains after suppression, not the total glucocorticoid effect experienced by the organism.
- Dexamethasone itself contributes the suppressive signal but differs from cortisol in MR engagement and BBB penetration (S023).
- Therefore “low post-dex cortisol” does not imply “cortisol-equivalent brain state achieved” and certainly does not imply PFC enhancement.

IMPORTANT ASSAY/INTERPRETATION DETAILS
- In the Genere study, dexamethasone adequacy was defined as >=0.1 mcg/dL and was met in most participants (S021).
- Total cortisol between 1.8 and 5 mcg/dL contained all false positives relative to free cortisol, especially in OCP users (S021).
- This shows that even endocrine interpretation requires simultaneous consideration of dexamethasone exposure and free-vs-total cortisol.

RED-FLAG MISINTERPRETATIONS TO AVOID
A) “Post-dex cortisol equals the blood cortisol equivalent of dexamethasone.” -> FALSE
B) “Suppressed cortisol means the brain got a cortisol-like GR/MR state.” -> FALSE
C) “DST data can be converted into PFC function predictions.” -> NOT SUPPORTED
D) “A 0.5 mg dex dose can be translated to a single PFC-relevant cortisol level.” -> NOT SUPPORTED

TRACEABILITY LABELS
Direct: DST interpretation affected by dex absorption/metabolism and altered binding [S021]
Direct: dexamethasone acts strongly at pituitary feedback sites [S023; S009]
Synthesis: therefore suppression outcomes cannot serve as cognitive equivalence outcomes.

PROVISIONAL TAKEAWAY
DST logic is about suppression testing, not cognitive replacement. The user’s explicit rule is supported by the opened evidence: post-dex cortisol must not be treated as a cortisol-equivalent effect.
