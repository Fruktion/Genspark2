EVIDENCE EXTRACTION TABLE

Study: S003
Citation: Henckens et al. 2011 PNAS
Species: Human
Sex: Male only
Health state / model: Healthy young volunteers
Endogenous glucocorticoid context: Normal baseline HPA axis; afternoon testing to reduce diurnal variation
Intervention compound: Hydrocortisone
Dose: 10 mg
Route: Oral capsule
Timing relative to testing: 30 min before n-back (rapid condition) or 240 min before n-back (slow condition)
Acute vs chronic: Acute single dose
Comparator: Placebo; between-group randomized double-blind design
Whether cortisol measured: Yes
Assay type: Salivary cortisol, repeated time points, immunoassay collection via Salivette (full text opened)
Cognitive task: Numerical n-back (0-3 back) during fMRI
Plausibly PFC-dependent: Yes, dorsolateral PFC/working-memory task
Direction of effect: Rapid condition null; slow condition modest improvement in combined WM metric and increased DLPFC activity
Normalization / enhancement / impairment / mixed / null: Mixed, with timing-dependent enhancement signal for slow effects rather than a uniform effect
Major confounders: Men only; behavioral effect modest; pharmacological hydrocortisone lacks natural stress co-mediators; no dexamethasone arm
Source-access grade: FULL_TEXT_OPENED
Evidence-strength note: Important direct PFC-relevant study showing timing matters and undermining one-dose/one-cognitive-effect thinking.

Study: S002
Citation: Wingenfeld et al. 2011 Psychopharmacology
Species: Human
Sex: Mixed healthy controls and MDD patients
Health state / model: Healthy subjects contrasted with major depressive disorder
Endogenous glucocorticoid context: Acute oral hydrocortisone challenge
Intervention compound: Hydrocortisone
Dose: 10 mg
Route: Oral
Timing relative to testing: Before Word Suppression Test
Acute vs chronic: Acute
Comparator: Placebo
Whether cortisol measured: Yes, saliva mentioned in abstract
Assay type: Not fully extracted; abstract-level only
Cognitive task: Word Suppression Test with neutral and negative interference words
Plausibly PFC-dependent: Yes, verbal working memory / interference control with emotional interference component
Direction of effect: Healthy subjects performed worse after hydrocortisone when negative interference words were present; MDD patients showed no effect
Normalization / enhancement / impairment / mixed / null: Mixed across populations; impairment in healthy controls, null in MDD
Major confounders: Emotional interference condition appears critical; abstract only; disease-state receptor-function interpretation remains inferential
Source-access grade: ABSTRACT_ONLY
Evidence-strength note: Useful for task- and population-dependence; not sufficient alone for strong quantitative claims.

Study: S014
Citation: Porter et al. 2002 J Psychopharmacology
Species: Human
Sex: Men and women
Health state / model: Healthy elderly subjects
Endogenous glucocorticoid context: Normal aging population
Intervention compound: Hydrocortisone
Dose: 20 mg twice, 12 h and 1 h before testing
Route: Oral/administration abstract does not specify otherwise; treated as administered doses in clinical trial context
Timing relative to testing: 12 h and 1 h pre-test
Acute vs chronic: Short-term acute regimen
Comparator: Placebo, double-blind crossover
Whether cortisol measured: Yes
Assay type: Salivary cortisol; abstract reports ~10-fold elevation at testing
Cognitive task: Battery including attention, working memory, declarative memory, executive function
Plausibly PFC-dependent: Partly yes for executive/working-memory components
Direction of effect: No significant effects found
Normalization / enhancement / impairment / mixed / null: Null
Major confounders: Elderly population may differ in receptor sensitivity; full task-specific details not yet extracted from full text
Source-access grade: ABSTRACT_ONLY
Evidence-strength note: Important disconfirming evidence against simple serum-cortisol elevation implying cognitive change.

Study: S015
Citation: Young et al. 1999 Psychopharmacology
Species: Human
Sex: Normal male volunteers
Health state / model: Healthy intact men
Endogenous glucocorticoid context: Chronic exogenous hydrocortisone exposure superimposed on intact HPA axis
Intervention compound: Hydrocortisone
Dose: 20 mg twice daily for 10 days
Route: Oral/administration context implies oral clinical dosing
Timing relative to testing: During/after 10-day administration period
Acute vs chronic: Chronic short-term repeated dosing
Comparator: Placebo crossover
Whether cortisol measured: Urine mentioned in MeSH; exact assay details not yet extracted
Assay type: Not fully extracted
Cognitive task: CANTAB subtests including spatial working memory, paired associates, Tower of London
Plausibly PFC-dependent: Yes for spatial working memory and strategy use; mixed hippocampal/PFC for paired associates
Direction of effect: Impairment in visuo-spatial memory and strategy use; more paired-associate errors; no Tower of London effect
Normalization / enhancement / impairment / mixed / null: Predominantly impairment with some null outcomes
Major confounders: Healthy men only; abstract only; chronic exposure not physiological replacement
Source-access grade: ABSTRACT_ONLY
Evidence-strength note: Strongly relevant against enhancement-generalization in healthy intact humans.

Study: S020
Citation: Schlosser et al. 2013 Psychiatry Research
Species: Human
Sex: Men and women in healthy controls plus MDD inpatients
Health state / model: Healthy controls and major depressive disorder
Endogenous glucocorticoid context: Acute challenge
Intervention compound: Hydrocortisone (described as acute cortisol administration)
Dose: 10 mg hydrocortisone
Route: Oral
Timing relative to testing: Before emotional go/no-go task
Acute vs chronic: Acute
Comparator: Placebo, double-blind crossover
Whether cortisol measured: Saliva mentioned in MeSH/abstract context
Assay type: Not fully extracted from full text
Cognitive task: Emotional go/no-go with fearful/happy/neutral faces
Plausibly PFC-dependent: Yes, response inhibition / executive control with affective context
Direction of effect: Enhancing effect on inhibitory performance in healthy controls (faster responses), no effect in MDD
Normalization / enhancement / impairment / mixed / null: Mixed, task-specific enhancement in healthy controls but not broad enhancement claim
Major confounders: Emotional task may recruit affective salience networks; abstract only; faster responses do not automatically equal globally better cognition
Source-access grade: ABSTRACT_ONLY
Evidence-strength note: Demonstrates outcome-discipline necessity: some inhibition tasks may improve while WM can worsen or stay unchanged.

INTERIM PATTERN SUMMARY
- Human hydrocortisone effects are not uniform.
- Working memory/PFC-like performance can worsen (S002), improve depending on timing (S003), or show no change despite major salivary cortisol elevation (S014).
- Chronic repeated hydrocortisone in healthy men can impair frontal-lobe-sensitive tasks (S015).
- Some inhibitory control outcomes may improve acutely in healthy controls (S020), which should not be generalized to all cognition or all PFC functions.

PROVISIONAL INFERENCE (NOT A SOURCE)
Hydrocortisone/cortisol administration does not support a single directional conclusion such as "more cortisol improves thinking" or "more cortisol impairs thinking". The human evidence is timing-, task-, population-, and chronicity-dependent.
