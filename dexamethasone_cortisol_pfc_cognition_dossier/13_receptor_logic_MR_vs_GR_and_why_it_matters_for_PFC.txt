MR-GR BASICS
- MR has higher affinity for endogenous glucocorticoids and is heavily occupied at basal levels.
- GR has lower affinity and becomes more engaged during circadian peaks or stress-related rises (S023).
- The balance, not simply the absolute amount of steroid, shapes neural and behavioral adaptation (S023).

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR PFC
- PFC functions are stress-sensitive and nonlinear; excessive or deficient glucocorticoid signaling can contribute to executive dysfunction, especially in interaction with catecholamine signaling (S011).
- Timing-sensitive hydrocortisone effects on DLPFC working memory (S003) are compatible with receptor-state dependence rather than a monotonic “more is better” model.
- Rhythm-dependent hydrocortisone replacement effects (S022) further imply that receptor engagement over time matters.

DEXAMETHASONE-SPECIFIC RECEPTOR PROBLEM
- Dexamethasone is a potent GR ligand but weak MR substitute in brain contexts (S023).
- Because endogenous cortisol/corticosterone normally provide substantial MR tone, dexamethasone cannot be assumed to recreate the same receptor balance.
- When dexamethasone suppresses endogenous glucocorticoid production, brain MR-rich states may be reduced rather than replaced, yielding a fundamentally different neuroendocrine context (S023).

CONSEQUENCE FOR EQUIVALENCE CLAIMS
A conversion that matches anti-inflammatory potency or even endocrine suppression does not ensure matched MR:GR balance in the brain. Since PFC outcomes are sensitive to receptor balance, any single dexamethasone-to-cortisol cognitive conversion is undercut at the receptor level.

CLAIM STATUS
- “MR and GR are biologically distinct and jointly relevant” -> SUPPORTED [S023]
- “PFC effects can be inferred from total glucocorticoid potency alone” -> DISCONFIRMED / NOT SUPPORTED [S011, S023]
- “Dexamethasone recreates endogenous MR-rich brain conditions” -> DISCONFIRMED [S023]

TRACEABILITY
Direct source-backed statement: dexamethasone has little MR affinity and poor BBB penetration (S023).
Synthesis: therefore matched serum values or dose tables cannot guarantee PFC-equivalent receptor conditions.

PROVISIONAL TAKEAWAY
Receptor logic strongly argues against collapsing dexamethasone and cortisol into a single cognitive-equivalence scale. For PFC questions, MR/GR patterning is a central reason conversion fails.
