TITLE: How to read INCI without pseudoscience
PURPOSE: Teach a practical, evidence-based way to interpret cleanser INCI lists.
STATUS: UPDATED_WITH_WORKED_EXAMPLE
SCOPE: Wash-off facial cleansers in the Polish market dossier.
DEPENDENCIES: 07_glossary_and_prerequisites.txt, 22_surfactant_taxonomy_and_irritancy.txt
LAST_UPDATED: 2026-03-31

CORE RULES
1. Do not rank cleansers by one “hero ingredient”.
2. In wash-off products, surfactant system, fragrance burden, and overall formula simplicity usually matter more than the presence of ceramides or plant extracts.
3. “Natural” is not a synonym for low-irritation.
4. A scary-sounding ingredient is not automatically harsh; context matters.
5. Unknown pH is still unknown even if marketing says “balanced”.

WORKED EXAMPLE A: CeraVe Nawilżająca Emulsja do Mycia
Observed INCI highlights:
- Glycerin
- fatty alcohols (cetearyl alcohol, stearyl alcohol, cetyl alcohol)
- ceramides, cholesterol, phytosphingosine
- sodium lauroyl lactylate
- no added fragrance claim
How to read it:
- This is not a classic foaming gel. It is a cream-emulsion cleanser.
- Barrier-supportive lipids are plausible positives, but because rinse-off contact is short, the main practical advantage is that the cleanser system is not aggressive.
- The likely trade-off is weaker one-step removal of heavy SPF/makeup.

WORKED EXAMPLE B: BasicLab Normalizujący żel oczyszczający
Observed INCI highlights:
- Lauryl Glucoside + Sodium Cocoamphoacetate
- no added fragrance claim
- several anti-inflammatory / seboregulating actives
How to read it:
- The key is the mild gel surfactant system and fragrance-free positioning, not the marketing actives.
- Better cleansing adequacy is plausible than with a very creamy lotion cleanser.
- It may still not be ideal for the most reactive users because glucoside-family surfactants can still sting some people.

WHAT TO DE-EMPHASIZE
- tiny amounts of botanical extracts near the end of INCI
- “dermatologically tested” without context
- “hypoallergenic” without robust support
- dramatic claims based on one barrier lipid ingredient

WHEN INCI IS NOT ENOUGH
- when reformulation is possible
- when pH is central to the user’s decision but not explicitly published
- when a user has known allergy to CAPB, glucosides, fragrance, MI, or formaldehyde releasers
- when heavy sunscreen/makeup removal is the key use case
