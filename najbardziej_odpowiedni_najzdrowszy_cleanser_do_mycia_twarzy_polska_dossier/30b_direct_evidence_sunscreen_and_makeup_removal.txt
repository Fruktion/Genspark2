KEY SOURCE
A10: The optimal cleansing method for the removal of sunscreen: Water, cleanser or cleansing oil? PMID 31157512.
ACCESS_GRADE: ABSTRACT_ONLY
WHAT IT SUPPORTS
- Water alone was not enough for robust sunscreen removal.
- A cleanser and especially cleansing oil improved removal performance.
- Dry skin adverse events were reported more often in the cleanser arm than in the cleansing-oil arm in the search summary already captured.

DIRECT DOSSIER IMPLICATIONS
- Ultra-mild lotion/emulsion cleansers cannot be assumed to remove heavy water-resistant sunscreen or makeup well in one pass.
- Heavy SPF/makeup scenario must down-rank very weak cleansers on cleansing adequacy even if barrier tolerability is excellent.
- Double cleansing remains a boundary condition rather than a failure of a mild cleanser when used in the proper role.

EVIDENCE HONESTY NOTE
- Because full text was not opened, claims remain modest: this source is used to justify a caution, not a precise effect-size statement.

RANKING CONSEQUENCE
- For scenario 6 (heavy SPF/makeup users), creamy non-foaming cleansers are often best interpreted as second-cleansers or morning cleansers, not universal one-step winners.
