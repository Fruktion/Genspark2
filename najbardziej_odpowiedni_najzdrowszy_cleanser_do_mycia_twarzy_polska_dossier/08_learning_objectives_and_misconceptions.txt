TITLE: Learning objectives and misconceptions
PURPOSE: Clarify what the dossier should teach and what myths it must actively correct.
STATUS: UPDATED
SCOPE: Consumer and analyst understanding of facial cleansers for the Polish market.
DEPENDENCIES: 07_glossary_and_prerequisites.txt, 08b_how_to_read_INCI_without_pseudoscience.txt
LAST_UPDATED: 2026-03-31

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
- Understand why “healthiest cleanser” is scenario-dependent.
- Understand why barrier/tolerability and cleansing adequacy must both be scored.
- Understand how to read INCI without hero-ingredient bias.
- Understand why fragrance-free helps but does not solve every problem.
- Understand why explicit product verification matters more than reputation alone.
- Understand why users on retinoids, benzoyl peroxide, or isotretinoin often need a gentler cleanser than they expect.

MISCONCEPTIONS TO CORRECT
- “Low pH automatically means mild.” Wrong; surfactant system matters (A06, A01).
- “If a product says hypoallergenic, it must be safest.” Wrong; claim-governance and proof burden matter (G01, G03).
- “Ceramides in a cleanser guarantee barrier repair.” Overstated; rinse-off benefit is limited and mostly indirect.
- “The gentlest cleanser is best for everyone.” Wrong; heavy SPF/makeup and oily/acne-prone users may need more cleansing adequacy.
- “Preservative-free is automatically safer.” Wrong; specific allergens matter more than preservative panic.
- “Natural essential oils are gentle.” Often false for reactive/allergy-suspect users.

RETINOID / BPO / ISOTRETINOIN MINI-SECTION
- During retinoid or benzoyl peroxide use, skin often behaves like a reactive-barrier-impaired scenario even if baseline skin is oily.
- Isotretinoin users often need to temporarily up-rank creamy fragrance-free emulsions/lotion cleansers.
- If stinging or burning appears, first suspect cleanser harshness, frequency, rubbing technique, or over-cleansing rather than assuming the active medication alone is at fault.

TROUBLESHOOTING MINI-SECTION
If cleanser causes stinging:
- check fragrance / essential oils / harsh actives / personal allergen history
- reduce rubbing and water temperature
- test a simpler creamy cleanser
If skin feels tight after washing:
- likely cleansing is too aggressive for the current barrier state
If sunscreen still feels present after washing:
- upgrade cleansing adequacy or use double cleansing instead of assuming the cleanser is “bad”.
